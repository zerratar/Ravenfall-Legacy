## Your character keeps everything

Levels, items, coins, all of it. What changes is how a fight plays out. The game shows a few pages about it the first
time you start it (and again from Settings > Interface > What's new), and the website has the full story:
[How combat works](https://www.ravenfall.stream/combat), [Skill trees](https://www.ravenfall.stream/skill-trees),
[The tower](https://www.ravenfall.stream/tower), [Battle pets](https://www.ravenfall.stream/pets).

## The new combat

### Health is now Vitality
The Health skill is called **Vitality**. It still grows by itself from all your combat training (a third of your combat
experience), and `!train all` still trains Attack, Defense and Strength together. Your health is 8 per Vitality level plus
what your armor pieces add, so the numbers look different from before. Nothing was taken away.

### Armor takes damage off
Armor no longer makes blows miss. It takes a share off every hit, so hits land, fights last, and heavy armor matters.

### Roles and threat
Enemies hit whoever worries them most. Damage builds threat; the Threat attribute, the Bulwark tree, Taunt and fighting
in melee with a one-handed sword build more, so a tank can hold them while Healing keeps the others standing and
everyone else deals the damage. A tank in front matters, most of all against bosses. Raid bosses now say which element
they strike with, and clicking an enemy shows what it is weak to and what it resists.

### Five elements
Physical, fire, ice, lightning and poison. Weapons and gems can carry element damage, enemies strike with elements too,
and each enemy shrugs off some and is weak to others. Fire burns, ice chills (five chills freeze), lightning jumps to the
next enemy and can stun, poison stacks. A word over a fighter shows when an effect starts (Burning, Frozen, Immune);
turn it off in Settings > Interface > Effect words.

### Skill trees
Your levels give you skill points: your combat levels, Taming, and the trade skills too (see below). Spend them with `!learn` (or plan a build on the website), put active skills in
your loadout with `!loadout`, and your character uses them on its own in fights. `!tree` shows your points, `!respec`
gives them back: the first time free, then for a Tome of Forgetting (every weekly quest and finished quest chain gives
one) or coins. `!tree cleave` tells you about a skill: when your character uses it and which weapon it needs.

Each tree ends in **three capstones** for a maxed skill (level 999), 15 points each, and **you can learn only one of
a tree's three**: its ending. The first is the all-rounder (Unbreakable, Miracle Worker, Colossus, Perfect Form,
Deadeye, Archmage, Undying, Legend of the Hunt, and Pack Leader for Taming's tree, Beastmaster). The other two are
paths that ask for the skills of their theme below them, so you build toward one:
- **Precision**: First Blood (the Assassin: your first hit on a new target is a critical hit through part of its
  armor, and your crits poison) or Flowing Steel (the Blade Dancer: faster with every hit in a row on one target).
- **Might**: Blood Rage (the Berserker: the less health you have, the harder you hit, and your blows heal you) or
  Aftershock (the Earthshaker: every fourth hit strikes everything around you).
- **Bulwark**: Shield of the Many (the Guardian: you take part of the blows meant for allies near you) or
  Retribution (the Avenger: thorns, and a much harder hit after every block).
- **Restoration**: Holy Fire (the Crusader: your heals also strike an enemy) or Guardian Angel (the Lifebinder: once
  a minute an ally near you who would die is saved).
- **Marksman**: Clean Kill (the Sniper: far more damage to healthy enemies) or Volley (the Stormbow: every third
  shot hits a second enemy).
- **Elements**: Wildfire (the Pyromancer: much stronger burns that jump on when an enemy dies) or Eye of the Storm
  (the Stormcaller: lightning hits harder with every jump, shorter cooldowns).
- **Vitality**: Crimson Pact (the Blood Drinker: life steal, and what you do not need becomes a shield) or
  Ironblood (fewer critical hits on you, far more healing received).
- **Hunter**: Thin the Herd (the Culler: more damage to everything that is not a boss, more still after a kill) or
  Fortune's Favorite (nothing in a fight, a lot more loot from raids and dungeons).
- **Beastmaster**: Alpha (one larger, stronger pet and no packmate) or Trailblazer (a much better gatherer pet).

`!tree precision` lists a tree's three and which one you chose, `!tree assassin` explains one, and `!learn assassin`
(the path's name) or `!learn first blood` (the capstone's) learns it. A respec gives the choice back. The website's
map shows the three as crowns at the end of each wedge. Loot luck (Treasure Hunter, Legend of the Hunt, Fortune's
Favorite) gives you more items from raids and dungeons.

### Trade skill trees: Gathering and Artisan
Gatherers and crafters have trees of their own. Woodcutting, Mining, Fishing, Farming, Gathering, Crafting, Cooking,
Alchemy and Sailing levels now earn skill points too, into the same pool as your combat levels (up to 97 more with all
nine at 999) and without touching your combat level. Two new trees spend them, on the **Trade** tab of the
[planner](https://www.ravenfall.stream/skill-trees) and of your character's Skill trees tab:

- **Gathering** (Woodcutting, Mining, Fishing, Farming, Gathering): Sure Hands (better odds on every find), Village's
  Share (now and then one more for the village), Bountiful (a chance that a find is two), Tireless (a chance that a
  round counts twice), Quick Return (every find comes round again sooner), Nest Finder (now and then a Chick, Kitten or
  Piglet Egg while you gather: pets that gather), and at 999 Master of the Trade.
- **Artisan** (Crafting, Cooking, Alchemy): Steady Hands (fewer burned meals), Thrifty (a chance that making something
  uses up nothing), Second Helping (a chance of one more meal or potion), Gem Cutter (combining gems costs up to 30%
  less), Gem Salvager (needs Crafting 300), Enchanter's Patience (a shorter wait
  between enchants), and at 999 Master Artisan.

They are all passives: nothing to put in a loadout, they work by themselves while you gather and make things. A skill
opens once one of its tree's skills is at its level, and then works in each of them that is. `!tree gathering` and
`!tree artisan` list them, `!tree` says where your points come from, and a respec gives back these points with the rest.

### Gems and sockets
Raw gems come from mining and gathering, more from the tower, raids and dungeons. Your clan enchants them, `!socket` puts
one into your gear for stats or an element, `!gems combine` turns three into a better one, `!unsocket` takes one out. A gem
taken out, or socketed over, usually crumbles: you are told your chance to keep it first and add `confirm` to go ahead.

### Class gear
Armor for your path. Thirteen new lines of gear give up some armor for what your role needs:

- **Mage** robes: magic power, cast speed and accuracy.
- **Healer** vestments: healing power, heals that also shield, shorter cooldowns and more health.
- **Ranger** leathers: ranged power, critical hits, attack speed and evasion.
- **Melee** mail: melee power, critical damage, attack speed and armor penetration.
- **Tank** plate: the armor of plate with more health, more threat and less damage taken.
- **Rogue** leathers (Attack): critical hits, attack speed, melee power and evasion.
- **Barbarian** hides (Strength): a lot of melee power and critical damage, and more health.
- **Samurai** armor (Attack or Strength): melee power, accuracy, armor penetration and critical hits.
- **Paladin** plate (Defense or Healing): health, healing power, threat and less damage taken.
- **Dark Knight** plate (Defense or Strength): life steal, thorns, melee power and health.
- **Witch Hunter** coats (Ranged): ranged power, critical hits, and more damage to bosses and the undead.
- **Monk** wraps (Healing): healing power, shorter cooldowns, health that comes back in a fight, more health and evasion.
- **Beastmaster** furs and hides (Taming): for those who fight beside a battle pet. Your pet hits and heals harder,
  has more health and is back on its feet sooner, a gatherer pet finds more, and taming is likelier. You get more
  health and critical hits with any weapon. No power bonus of your own: with a pet out it keeps up with a damage
  set, without one it does not. The first gear that Taming levels open.

Eight tiers each, from level 1 to 900 (Apprentice to Starcaller, Guard to Aegis, and so on), with two head pieces a
tier to choose your look from. They are made with Crafting from two new materials and a bar or more of metal: **Linen**,
woven from **Flax** (a new Farming crop: aim for it with `!farm flax`), and **Leather**, tanned from **Hide** (found now and then while you train a
combat skill). `!req sorcerer robe` tells you what a piece takes.

Some of the higher tiers wear a cloak, a pack or a pelt on the back. A cape takes that place while you wear one, your
own or your clan's: `!toggle cape` hides your cape and shows it again, just as `!toggle helmet` does for your helmet.

Gear that shows skin, like the Barbarian's harness or a pair of fingerless gloves, shows your character's own skin
color.

`!equip all` knows your path: gear made for the skill you are training wins over plain armor of its level, and gear
made for another path is not put on by accident. Go fishing or chop wood for a while and what you wear stays on. Where
two lines fit what you train (a Tank's and a Paladin's for Defense), the one you wear stays until you change it.

**An outfit for every role.** `!equip tank`, `!equip melee`, `!equip ranger`, `!equip mage` and `!equip healer` put on
your best class gear for that role, whatever you are training (a skill's name works too: `!equip defense`, `attack`,
`strength`, `ranged`, `magic`, `healing`). A line's name wears just that line: `!equip rogue`, `!equip barbarian`,
`!equip samurai`, `!equip paladin`, `!equip dark knight`, `!equip witch hunter`, `!equip monk`, and `!equip plate` for
the metal armor. The outfit stays on, also when something else turns up in your bag, until you use `!equip all`.

**Your outfit follows what you train.** What you wore the last time you trained a skill comes back when you train it
again: your robes for Magic, your plate for Defense, whatever you like for Fishing, and the battle pet you had out
with it. You do nothing for it. The first time you leave a skill, what you are wearing is kept as its outfit, and
until you have worn different gear for different skills nothing changes at all. Join a raid, a dungeon or a tower
floor while you are gathering and you fight in the outfit of the combat skill you trained last; when it is over
your gathering outfit is back on. Gear never changes in the middle of a fight. If a piece of an outfit is gone (sold,
sent, stashed), the best you own takes its place and chat tells you once.

- `!outfit` shows the outfit of what you train. `!outfit save` keeps what you wear now for it.
- `!outfit pin` also stops better gear from replacing it, for the outfit you love the look of. `!outfit unpin` lets
  better gear in again.
- `!outfit mining` (any skill, or `all`) puts that skill's outfit on now.
- `!outfit auto off` if you would rather change by hand, `!outfit upgrade off` if you never want gear put on by
  itself. Both are on unless you turn them off.

A better piece you find also goes into the outfits you are not wearing, where it fits, so they are up to date when
you come back to them. What `!equip tank` and its relatives put on becomes the outfit of what you are training: train
something else and come back, and it is still your tank gear, until `!equip all`.

**Gear for tamers.** The Beastmaster line is worn by choice, since no combat skill calls for it: `!equip
beastmaster` (or `!equip tamer`) puts on your best set, `!outfit pin` keeps it as a skill's outfit. Its stats show in
the pet view of the details window ("Power from you"), and `!tame` tells you the chance with it in. Fighters with a pet
out sometimes find a Beastmaster piece in a tower chest instead of their own line's.

**The tower gives it too.** The first time your stream clears a part of the tower, everyone who fought finds a piece of class
gear in the chest: of a line that fits the skill you train, as high a tier as the floor's level and your own allow,
and with a gem of that part of the tower already in it (an Emerald in the Overgrown Halls, a Sapphire in the Frozen
Vault, a Topaz in the Storm Spire). The higher the floor, the better the gem. Chat says what you found: "Sorcerer Robe
with a Flawed Sapphire".

### Dark Fortress gear

New weapons, shields and helmets from a dark fortress:

- **Dreadforged** weapons and a shield (level 450), crafted from Atlarus bars and an Elder Iron bar: a sword, a
  morningstar, a cleaver, a battleaxe, a staff and a shield. Each has a little extra, such as life steal or thorns.
- **Rare finds** in the hardest dungeon and tower chests: the Gravebloom Warhammer, the Sunstaff of the Inquisitor and
  the Skullcrusher. Two cosmetic finds too: the Thorn Crown and the Grinning Skull.
- **New helmets for the high tiers of six gear lines**: the Fortress Helm (Dark Knight), Closed Helm (Paladin),
  Inquisitor Helm (Witch Hunter), Kettle Helm (Melee), Antler Crown (Barbarian) and Lich Crown (Mage). They work with
  transmogrification like any head piece.
- **Fortress armor for three gear lines**: the top two tiers of the Dark Knight, Barbarian and Mage lines look like
  the fortress's own Knight, Barbarian and Necromancer (Voidknight and Abyssal, Stormborn and Worldbreaker, Voidweaver
  and Starcaller), with the helmets above to match. Bare arms and hands show your own skin color. Any armor piece can
  take the look by transmogrification.
- **Fortress pieces for four more lines**, at their top two tiers: plate shoulder guards for Melee, a fur mantle for
  the Beastmaster, spiked pauldrons and spiked boots for the Rogue, and a mossy fur collar with skulls and spiked
  bracers for the Witch Hunter.

### Legendary weapons

Five fortress weapons are **Legendary**, the best weapons in the game: the **Soulreaver**, the **Hellhorn Mace**, the
**Batwing Staff**, the **Spiked Bulwark** and the **Fortress Hand Cannon** (a bow that fires cannonballs, with a muzzle
flash, smoke and a boom). They stay the best: each is worked out from the best normal weapon of its kind, plus a
margin, so when better gear comes the Legendaries rise with it.

- **Who can find one**: level 950 in the weapon's skill (Strength for the Hellhorn Mace), a clear on tower floor 3 or
  higher fought in (any of its parts), and your ending (capstone) chosen in that skill's tree. Then a boss on tower
  floor 3 (a mini boss or the floor boss), a Legendary dungeon's boss or a raid boss may drop one for you. Very rare, a little less rare every time it does not drop, and
  never the same one twice: once you own every one you can find, a find becomes a Legend's Echo (more awakening, or a
  Flawless gem once all are fully awakened).
- **A power of its own**: the Soulreaver heals you on every kill, the Hellhorn Mace freezes enemies in fear, the
  Batwing Staff sends swarms of bats, the Spiked Bulwark's thorns answer every blow and it shields the players around
  you, and the Hand Cannon's third shot is a blast that knocks the target back.
- **Awakening**: fight bosses with it on and it grows through five ranks: stronger numbers and power, a fourth and a
  fifth gem socket, and a glow in its own color (a setting, off in potato mode).
- Bound to whoever finds it: it cannot be traded, stashed, sent to your other characters or sold. Chat hears about a find, and the website's **Hall of Legends** shows who found each one
  first and who has fully awakened one. There are titles to find too.

### Enchanted gear shows lower numbers
An old bug counted POWER, AIM and ARMOR enchantments twice: a Lionsbane Katana (173 power) with POWER 20% showed 241
power instead of 207. That is fixed, so enchanted gear shows lower numbers than in 0.9.6. Nothing about the item changed;
the old number was wrong.

## The tower

A climb for the whole stream: `!tower` takes you in. The tower has **floors**, each a world of its own with a town,
and every floor has **parts**. Three floors of ten parts are open: the Overgrown Halls, the Frozen Vault and the Storm
Spire above the clouds, with more floors to come.

- **Objectives that count by themselves.** Every part has objectives for the whole stream, and nothing asks anyone to
  click or hand something in: its monsters defeated by whoever trains there, materials the stream's players gather
  wherever they train (counted, never taken from your bag; monsters defeated on the part count too, so a stream of
  fighters is never stuck), and hidden things that whoever trains on the part finds after about three minutes of
  fighting. The townspeople ask for them: Warden Elm wants timber for the stair, Old Tove wants fish for the winter.
  A part takes five players about 25 minutes (a floor's first part about 20, its last about 30), so a stream clears
  a few in an evening.
- **Monsters that come for you.** A part's monsters stand in packs of two or three, wander about, and walk over to
  attack a player who comes near them: on the first floor only close by and one at a time, higher up from further
  away and up to three on one player. A tank's blows still pull them off the others. Players go for the monsters the
  part still needs most.
- **Mini bosses and floor bosses.** When a part's objectives are done its guardian can be challenged (`!tower boss`).
  Most are mini bosses, with a short intro and a quick "Part 7 cleared". The last part of a floor has the **floor
  boss**: the big intro and fall, the world first for the first stream to beat it, the next floor and its town. Whoever
  lands the last blow on a floor boss earns the title "the Finisher" (blows that land together share it).
- **Level sync.** On a part nobody fights above the part's level, so a stream of veterans cannot flatten a boss. A
  character far below a part's level is told so on the way in. The first floor is made for levels 10 to 100, the
  second for 115 to 250 and the third for 265 to 400, so a stream levels up before it climbs higher.
- **Climbing pays.** On a part your combat skills and Healing never earn less than they would on your island: a boss
  fight, its countdown, getting back up after a knock-out and the walk to the next monster included. And every floor
  pays 5% more on top: 5% on the first floor, 10% on the second, 15% on the third, shown on the tower panel and the
  floor map. The towns pay what your island does.
- **The fights follow the group.** A part is made for five players, a floor boss for eight. With more in the fight,
  or a full group made stronger by skill trees and gems, monsters and bosses have more health, bosses call more
  helpers and hit a little harder. A smaller group meets a smaller fight: for two players a mini boss on floor 1 has
  about half its health and softer blows. Floor 1 follows the group only a little, every floor above it more. In a
  small group your trees and gems are all your own, and veterans never have worse odds than fresh players: they just
  finish sooner.
- **A town on every floor** (Mossgate, Rimeholt and Galewharf, a harbor on the clouds): people to talk to, quests, a campfire to rest at, and a place to
  train every skill as on your island.
- **Cleared parts stay open.** A part your stream has cleared is a place to train, like an island: `!tower part 3`
  takes you there (only you; `!tower 1-3` names the floor too, `!tower floor 1` goes to a floor's latest part) while
  the climb goes on, and `!tower` brings you back to it. Up to three parts are open at once (one in potato mode); a
  part nobody is on closes again. The streamer or a vote can still take the climbing group along with
  `!tower all part 3`, and back with `!tower all`; players who went to a part by themselves stay there.
- **Knocked out is not out.** In a boss fight a knocked out player gets up by the entrance, recovers for 40 seconds
  (it says so over them, and their health bar fills as they wait) and walks back into the fight at half health. The
  attempt is lost when nobody stands at the same moment, or when the time runs out, and then the game says so:
  "Defeated", why, and what the guardian had left. The guardians strike harder to make up for it, so a group of five
  can still lose (more often the higher the floor), and a floor boss is a longer fight than a mini boss.
- **The streamer's buttons.** The tower panel has **Challenge** when the guardian is open and **Start now** during
  the countdown (`!tower boss now` in chat, for the streamer and moderators).
- **Meters in the tower.** The dungeon's meters window opens for every boss fight in the tower, with the result after
  it (and who landed the last blow on a floor boss); the panel's Meters button shows them for the part you train on.
- **The players list follows the camera.** In the tower it lists the players on that part (or in that town), with who
  is knocked out or recovering; one click shows everyone again.
- **A map of the floor.** The tower panel's Map button (or `!tower map`) shows the floor: its town, its areas and
  their parts, lit as your stream clears them, with how many players are on each. The website's tower page has it
  for every floor some stream has reached.
- **Rooms and doors.** The crypt under the first floor and the ice vault of the second are fought room by room: each
  door stays sealed (thorns in the crypt, ice in the vault) until its room is cleared, the last room keeps filling up
  for training, and the way to the guardian opens when it is challenged.
- **A small stream is not a slow one.** A monster defeated counts for more toward a part's objectives when few players
  are on the part and for less when many are (the numbers on screen stay the same): two players need about 1.4 times
  the time of five, ten about 0.8 times, thirty about 0.6 times.
- **Chat decides when the streamer is away.** The streamer or a moderator decides at once; anyone in the tower can vote
  instead (`!tower boss`, `!tower climb`, `!tower all part 7`, `!tower close`). One voice opens a vote that passes a
  minute later, or at once when most of the players who are at their keyboards vote too (idle players do not count).
  `!tower close` still takes most of them. `!tower leave` takes only you out.
- **Raids and dungeons while you climb.** Auto join never pulls a climber out of the tower. If you want to go anyway,
  join by hand: `!raid join` takes you out of the tower and into the raid at once, and `!dungeon join` lets you keep
  climbing until the dungeon starts. The reply tells you to use `!tower` to go back in after. So some of the stream
  can climb while others raid.
- **Cutscenes:** the tower opening its gate, each new floor's town, each boss's first appearance with a taunt, and every
  boss falling in slow motion (short for a mini boss, the full one for a floor boss). Settings > Game > Observer camera >
  Cutscenes turns them off.
- **The dark fortress haunts the first floor:** Risen Dead, Grave Knights, Plague Walkers, Shambling Corpses,
  Crypt Wraiths and a few elites, and four new mini bosses: Mortimer Bonewhistle the Necromancer (part 4), Big Gorp the
  Keep Colossus (part 7), Lord Pestilus the Plague Lord (part 8) and Old Rotbelly the Behemoth (part 9). Beat one
  on your stream's first clear for a chance at its own weapon, shield or hat.
- **A tower panel on screen** while anyone is inside: the floor and part, its objectives and their progress, the boss
  that waits behind them, the boss countdown, the boss's health in a fight, the town between parts, and what to type next.
- **The tower stands in the world.** Far out in the sea south of Home, on its own islet, nearly 370 m of it before it
  rises into a deck of cloud: one section for each floor, the Overgrown Halls at its foot, then the Frozen Vault, the
  Storm Spire in its ring of thundercloud, and the Dark Fortress, dark until a floor of it opens. A floor your stream
  has cleared is lit, the floor you are on glows and breathes, and what you have not reached is dark. You see it from
  every island.
- **Sail to it:** `!sail tower` takes the ferry to the pier at its foot (the ferry only turns in there when someone
  asks for it), and your own boat can dock there too. There is nothing to train on the islet, but you keep earning
  experience as on your island. `!tower` from the plaza walks you in through the great gate.
- **Your stream's best climbers** are carved on the tall stone by the gate: the five players who fought in the most
  of your stream's clears, under the floor and part your stream is on and a carved tower that lights up floor by
  floor. The best of them stands on the pedestal as a statue in stone, in their own gear and a heroic pose for their
  weapon.
- **The camera looks at it:** when your stream beats a floor boss, a short cutscene follows its fall: the picture
  fades to the tower, the cleared floor flares, a band of light climbs to the next floor and lights it in its own
  color, and the title says the new floor is open, then it fades back to your players in the new floor's town (or
  straight into that town's first-visit cutscene). Skip or Esc ends it early. When the tower was empty and someone
  goes in, a short look from its gate; and now and then on a quiet stream, a slow look at it from wherever the
  camera is (about every quarter of an hour, a few seconds, and any key or mouse movement ends it). Settings > Game
  > Observer camera > Looks at the tower turns the last one off; the others follow Cutscenes.
- **The tower camera.** While anyone is in the tower the camera watches the group there by itself, with no single
  player followed: a boss fight with the guardian in the picture, else the part the climb is on, else the town. Press
  **T** (or the button on the tower panel or in the shortcut bar) to go out to the world, where the camera works as it
  always has and stays until you press T again. A player you click is followed as always. A raid, a dungeon or a
  cutscene hands the camera back to the side you were on, and when the last player leaves the tower it goes back
  outside. Rebind the key under Settings > Keys. Tree tops between the camera and the group fade away while they are
  in the way (Settings > Camera > Trees fade in the tower; off in potato mode).
- **Streamers who do not want it:** Settings > Game > World > Tower open to chat.

## Battle pets

Taming is a new skill. Pick a first pet with `!pets starter fox` (damage), `bear` (tank) or `fawn` (healer), tame
creatures you fight with `!tame bunny`, and hatch eggs from quests, raids, dungeons and the tower with `!pets hatch`. A pet
that is out fights beside you, levels up, learns skills and evolves. `!pets` shows yours. Clans have a new skill for them,
the Rookery. Taming stops when you tame a creature: one `!tame`, one pet, and `!tame` again for the next.

Every pet you already own can fight too: `!pets wake raven` (or just `!pets wake` for the one you wear). `!pet` still
picks your cosmetic pet, which shows whenever no battle pet is out.

**Taming has a skill tree: Beastmaster.** Its points come from the same pool as the combat trees, and Taming's levels
add to that pool (34 points at 999). Spend them on your pet instead of yourself: stronger and tougher pets that get
back up sooner, commands your character gives by itself (Sic 'Em, Mend Pet, Call of the Wild), one more skill slot,
a tank pet that takes more of your blows, a gatherer pet that finds more, and at Taming 999 a packmate that fights
beside your pet. `!tree beastmaster` lists it.

**Twenty-six kinds of pet hatch from eggs**, from the Wolf Pup and the Dragon Spark to eighteen animals: a panda that
sits down for a bamboo snack in the middle of a fight, a goose that HONKs, a penguin with snowballs, a spider that
webs enemies in place, a sheep that wraps you in wool, a capybara that heals by being calm, a pupper that grows into a
woofer, a pony that grows into a Nightmare, tigers, lions, storm eagles, parrots, reindeer, boars, rats, and a cat, a
truffle pig and a chicken that help you gather. Eggs turn up now and then in dungeons, raids, tower chests, quests and
island events, and each kind has its own places: penguins from the first floor boss of the tower on, parrots with the pirates, rats
and sheep on Ravebridge. The
[pets page](https://www.ravenfall.stream/pets) shows them all.

## Your character page on the website

- **Equipment** is a tab of its own, rebuilt: your character stands in the middle in 3D, wearing what it wears in
  the game, and tries a piece on when you point at it. Drag a piece onto its slot, double click it, or pick it and
  press Equip; a card beside every piece compares it with what you wear, and the numbers are the ones a fight in
  the game uses. There is an Equip best button too.
- **Outfits** live on the Equipment tab: the row of skills is each skill's outfit. Look at one that is not on, pin
  slots, add pieces, set its pet and put it on.
- **Inventory** is rebuilt too. It opens grouped by kind, the first two rows of each with "Show all", and a kind can
  be folded away. Pick big or small tiles, or **Everything** for one long grid; your page remembers what you chose. A
  search or a kind chip shows every match, and "Select several" stashes or sells many at once.
- **Gems**: socket a gem or take one out from an item's window.
- **The [Hall of Legends](https://www.ravenfall.stream/legends)** shows each Legendary's power, who found it first
  and who has fully awakened one, and the [tower page](https://www.ravenfall.stream/tower) has the map of every floor
  some stream has reached.

## Also new

- **Twelve new quest chains** (`!quest chains`), from Captain Whiskers to The magpie's ransom, five of them long ones for
  high levels, and more daily and weekly quests: the tower, Taming, crafting, cooking, brewing, and one for a skill you
  have neglected.
- **Experience books:** a quest that would pay experience in a skill you have at level 999 gives a book instead,
  worth what the quest would have paid: a Small book for a daily, a Medium one for a quest chain, a Large one for a
  weekly. `!use gathering book fishing` reads it for a skill of its kind (Combat, Gathering, Artisan or Adventure).
- **The daily streak now pays.** Daily quests have always shown a bonus for days in a row (10% more coins and
  experience a day, up to 50% after five days), but it was never paid out. Now it is.
- `!cook`, `!craft` and `!brew` only suggest things that skill can make when a name is not found.
- **Auto join for patrons:** patrons get some raids and dungeons auto joined free every day and a discount on the
  rest, more with each tier (Mithril 6 raids and 4 dungeons free and 10% off, up to Phantom 25 and 18 and 50% off).
- **Your own auto join limit:** `!auto limit 50k` caps what auto join may spend a day for all your characters
  together (`!auto limit off` removes it, `!auto limit` shows what it spent). Also on the website's dashboard, which
  now shows what auto join cost you today and this week.
- **Auto join leaves busy players alone:** players in the tower, on Ravebridge, in a war, a duel or a running arena
  are not pulled into a raid or dungeon by auto join, or charged for one.
- **Ravebridge opens when you open it.** The goblin uprisings that opened it by themselves now and then are off
  unless you turn them on (Settings > Game > World > Ravebridge uprisings; if you had turned them on, they stay on).
  Raids and dungeons now go on while Ravebridge is open, for the players who are not on it, and they no longer take
  the camera from the hero you are playing.
- **Townspeople are readable on stream:** what the people on the islands and in the tower's towns say is never too
  small to read, even with the camera far away, and two people standing close no longer talk over each other.
- **Hero mode:** the skill bar has a carved gold frame.
- **Messages in bottles:** the Message in a Bottle you fish up at sea can be opened now, `!use bottle`. Most of the
  time there is a note inside (from zerratar, who has more bottles, from islanders, about the islands, and the odd
  tip), and about one bottle in eight holds a **Treasure Map** to an island you can reach. Go there and
  `!use treasure map` to dig it up: coins, sometimes a gem, now and then a pet egg, and something rarer still.
- **Silly surprises:** the islands got a little stranger. Keep an eye out while you gather (some finds are odd, and
  `!use` and `!examine` have opinions about them), read the signs, watch the painter on the hill on Home, and do not
  plan anything serious for April 1st. A few new secret achievements hide in there too. Settings > Game > World >
  Silly surprises turns the visible ones off (they are always off in potato mode).

- Streams still on 0.9.6 keep running until they restart; a new session asks for the update.
